OnLabel

Checks pharma promo claims against the FDA label and shows the label text behind every verdict.

Try it: pick a claim in the copy

There's only one Ozempic. Ask your doctor about Ozempic.

Claim 1: Not in the labelunsupported absolute claim

The label lists Ozempic's approved uses and says nothing comparing their number with other GLP-1 drugs.

Claim 2: Traced to the label

Both parts are in the clinical studies section, quoted word for word.

Claim 3: Reviewer decidesthe model said traced

gpt-oss-120b called this traced. Its own quote limits the benefit to adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, and the claim leaves that population out, so a plain-code check sent it to the reviewer.

Whole piece: the copy has none of the boxed warning (risk of thyroid C-cell tumors), so the review says that first.

The live demo's Ozempic sample: synthetic test copy whose first lines echo claims FDA cited in a February 2026 untitled letter. Verdicts by gpt-oss-120b, checked by the guards.

Problem
Every pharma promotional asset goes through medical, legal and regulatory (MLR) review, where reviewers check by hand that each claim traces to the FDA label. ZS writes that approval takes 21 to 56 days per asset, and FDA sent more than 100 untitled and warning letters about drug ads in September 2025 alone.
What I built
Hybrid retrieval over 12 DailyMed labels (bge-small int8 embeddings on ONNX, BM25 that keeps figures whole, rank fusion, and routing to the sections that govern each claim), an open-weight LLM judge returning strict JSON with verbatim quotes, and plain-code guards that decide what is reported. Copy is read as untrusted input: hidden text is exposed and instructions aimed at the reviewer are caught before anything is traced.
Result
On a 132-claim benchmark, the production model traced 0 of 42 violative held-out claims with the guards (2 on its own) and 25 of 28 faithful ones. The governing label text reached the judge for 98% of claims, and no prompt-injection attack got its claim traced.
Product decision
Nothing is reported as traced unless plain-code checks find its quotes and figures in the label and the claim carries the conditions of the indication it quotes. Copy that tries to instruct the reviewer gets no traced claim at all. Uncertain cases go to the reviewer, whose decisions export as an audit file.

Python · FastAPI · ONNX Runtime · BM25 + bge-small · gpt-oss / Qwen / Gemma · React · Docker · Render

Mandate Retry Sequencer

Recovers failed UPI Autopay debits. An AI agent suggests retries and five compliance rules approve or block each one.

Try it: point at an hour

0 of 266 debits ran in a blocked hour.

NPCI peak window (10:00–13:00, 17:00–21:30)
Allowed; darker means more debits ran

Every debit from the committed run on synthetic data, by hour (IST).

Problem
Merchants retry failed debits, but can't easily show which retries were allowed, which were refused and what each one cost.
What I built
A LightGBM recoverability score, five hard compliance rules checked before and after every decision, and an LLM agent limited to the uncertain score band. Each decision is written to an append-only ledger.
Result
₹44.25L of ₹1.26Cr at risk recovered (35%) across 500 synthetic records. 187 records stopped by a rule, 11 agent proposals vetoed, 0 of 266 debits in an NPCI peak window. 214 tests.
Product decision
No score or agent proposal can override a rule. Every unrecovered record is listed on the dashboard, largest first.

Python · FastAPI · LightGBM · SHAP · Claude API · React · d3

PAD Detection + Risk Copilot

Predicts peripheral artery disease, then explains the score with sources you can check.

Try it: hover or tap a citation

Statins lower cholesterol and certain fats in the blood, and can slow the progression of plaque buildup in the arteries, which is a key cause of PAD NHLBI · PAD: Treatment · Medicinesretrieved passage · retrieval score 0.725.

Diabetes and hypertension are both risk factors for PAD, as they can damage blood vessels and increase the risk of atherosclerosis NHLBI · Peripheral Artery Disease: What Is PAD?retrieved passage · retrieval score 0.722.

A citation the model invents gets removed: StrippedNo passage 7 was retrieved, so the code removes it and flags the answer. Illustrative example from the README.

From a demo run of the copilot. The model in that run was trained on synthetic test data.

Problem
Someone reading a risk score needs to know why it came out that way, and where each claim in the explanation comes from.
What I built
A leakage-controlled pipeline over MIMIC-IV (13 features, 6 classifiers) and a local RAG copilot that explains each score with SHAP attributions and cited passages.
Result
Best AUC 0.88. The copilot retrieves the right passage for 24 of 24 golden questions, and no unresolvable citation survives. 54 tests.
Product decision
Each citation is checked in code against the retrieved passages. When nothing relevant is found, the copilot says it can't answer.

Python · scikit-learn · XGBoost · LightGBM · SHAP · Ollama · Streamlit

EpiGraph-AI

Forecasts dengue outbreaks across connected districts from cases, weather and news.

Problem
Outbreaks spread between connected districts and show up in local news before case counts, and single-signal forecasts miss both.
What I built
A hybrid GAT + LSTM model that fuses district connectivity, case and weather history, and BioBERT news embeddings, with a surveillance web app on top.
Result
R² 0.585, MAE 26.74 cases, and 63% outbreak detection accuracy (prediction within 50% or 10 cases of actual).
Product decision
I fixed the metrics before choosing the architecture, and used Huber loss so sudden case spikes don't dominate training.

A Comprehensive Analysis of Lightweight CNN for Fall Detection in Hallway Monitoring

Sherly Alphonse, Bhenedix Paul, Madhav K and Joshnavi Pokala. 10th International Conference on Inventive Systems and Control (ICISC), Coimbatore, pp. 890–896, published 5 August 2026. DOI 10.1109/ICISC69558.2026.11681696

Problem
Fall detection research mostly targets elderly care, where a system must tell a fall from lying down or sleeping. A school, hotel or office hallway needs one answer, fast: did someone fall?
What we built
A lightweight binary CNN (one sigmoid output, augmentation, dropout and batch norm) that runs on existing CCTV without a GPU, plus an email alert to reception or security when it detects a fall.
Result
Under 50 ms per frame on CPU. On 115 test samples: 70% accuracy and AUC 0.71; on the fall class, 0.82 precision and 0.66 recall.
Trade-off
It needs far less compute than R-CNN-style detectors, and gives up accuracy for it: GPU-based methods in the literature report around 90%. A single decision threshold can be tuned to catch more falls.
